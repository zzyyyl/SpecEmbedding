\begin{abstract}
Retrieving molecular structures from tandem mass spectra is difficult because
many candidates share mass, formula, and substructure information. Joint
embedding methods rank candidates by spectrum--molecule similarity, whereas
generative retrieval can introduce an autoregressive molecular decoder. We
investigate a third design point: direct, non-generative learning to rerank a
retrieved molecular list. Our framework, \method{}, first aligns a peak-sequence
spectrum encoder and a molecular graph encoder. A second-stage residual ranker
then combines spectrum and candidate embeddings, explicit interaction features,
the base score, and the base rank under listwise supervision. We compare a
context-free pointwise scorer with a candidate-set Transformer across three
reranker initializations while keeping the first-stage checkpoint and caches
fixed. On the 17,556-query MassSpecGym test split, their Recall@1 values are
$67.44\pm0.74$ and $68.58\pm0.20$ for mass-conditioned retrieval, and
$73.97\pm0.59$ and $74.57\pm0.28$ for formula-conditioned retrieval, compared
with fixed-base values of 47.46 and 63.10. The overlap-clean run gives the
Transformer a modest advantage, but the same paired-win pattern is absent under
an earlier alignment checkpoint. Thus, the current evidence strongly supports supervised
residual reranking while not establishing a checkpoint-robust benefit from
candidate self-attention. Both variants operate without autoregressive molecule
generation or test-time positive insertion.
\keywords{Data mining for bioinformatics \and Tandem mass spectrometry \and
Molecule retrieval \and Learning to rank \and Multimodal representation
learning}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) is widely used to identify small molecules in
metabolomics, natural-product discovery, and related biomedical applications.
An observed spectrum is only a partial, condition-dependent view of a molecular
structure: different molecules can yield similar fragments, while the same
molecule can produce different spectra across instruments, adducts, and
collision energies. Consequently, structure annotation is naturally a
large-scale data mining problem in which a query spectrum must retrieve and rank
a correct structure from a chemically constrained library.

MassSpecGym provides structure-disjoint data splits and standardized
mass- and formula-conditioned retrieval libraries for this problem
\cite{bushuiev2024massspecgym}. Recent methods mainly address the cross-modal
gap between spectra and molecules. JESTR learns a spectrum--molecule joint space
with contrastive multiview coding and ranks candidates directly by cosine
similarity \cite{kalia2025jestr,tian2020cmc}. GLMR instead retrieves contextual
candidates, generates a refined molecule with a conditional language model, and
reranks candidates by molecule--molecule similarity \cite{zhang2026glmr}.

Both approaches leave an important data mining question open. Fixed pairwise
cosine scores do not directly optimize the final candidate order, while
generative retrieval adds an autoregressive decoder and makes ranking depend on
the quality and validity of a generated structure. We ask whether hard
candidates can instead be separated directly in embedding space by a supervised
second-stage ranker, and whether explicit candidate interaction adds value beyond
a pointwise residual scorer.

We propose \method{}, a two-stage retrieval and reranking framework. The first
stage aligns a Transformer spectrum encoder with a GINE molecular graph encoder.
The second stage operates only on the first-stage top-$K$ list. It constructs
explicit spectrum--candidate interaction features, injects the original score
and rank as retrieval priors, and predicts a residual correction to the base
score. We instantiate this framework with pointwise and candidate-set
Transformer variants.

Our contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item We introduce a direct, non-generative residual learning-to-rank
  formulation for MS/MS-to-molecule retrieval, avoiding both fixed cosine-only
  ranking and autoregressive molecular decoding.
  \item We combine multimodal embeddings, symmetric interaction features, base
  scores, and rank embeddings under listwise and hard-negative pairwise
  supervision, and compare matched pointwise and self-attentive implementations
  while keeping the first-stage embeddings cacheable.
  \item Under MassSpecGym's official candidate protocol, both implementations
  substantially improve the fixed base retriever across three reranker seeds.
  The overlap-clean comparison finds a modest candidate-set Transformer
  advantage, but comparison across alignment checkpoints shows that this
  advantage is not yet robust enough to attribute the overall reranking gain to
  candidate self-attention.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Spectrum-to-Molecule Retrieval}

Earlier annotation systems compare an experimental spectrum with reference
spectra, predict a molecular fingerprint, or simulate a spectrum from each
candidate. Their coverage or accuracy is limited by reference libraries,
intermediate reconstruction error, or simulation quality. MIST predicts
structure-related fingerprints with formula-aware Transformers
\cite{goldman2023mist}; CMSSP aligns spectral and molecular representations by
contrastive pretraining \cite{chen2024cmssp}. These studies motivate direct
cross-modal retrieval but do not explicitly optimize a downstream candidate
list.

Our spectrum encoder originates from SpecEmbedding, which uses replicated
spectra and structural similarity in supervised contrastive learning
\cite{xiong2025specembedding}. In this work, it is paired with a molecular graph
encoder and optimized for cross-modal rather than spectrum-to-spectrum
retrieval. The aligned model used here is trained
from scratch, so the observed reranking gains do not rely on an external
pretrained spectrum checkpoint.

\subsection{JESTR: Joint-Space Cosine Ranking}

JESTR represents a molecule with a GCN and a spectrum with an MLP over
1-Da-binned peaks. Contrastive multiview coding aligns matched
spectrum--molecule pairs with an InfoNCE objective. During the final 3\% of
training, JESTR additionally pushes away PubChem molecules sharing the target
formula; its final score remains the cosine similarity between the query and
each candidate \cite{kalia2025jestr}. This design is simple and non-generative,
but candidates are scored independently and there is no learned downstream
ranking objective. The same-formula regularizer was also reported to provide
little or inconsistent improvement on the structure-disjoint MassSpecGym split,
which suggests that candidate-aware supervision alone is not sufficient unless
the ranking function can use it effectively.

\subsection{GLMR: Generation-Mediated Reranking}

GLMR first aligns a Transformer spectrum encoder with a frozen ChemFormer
molecule encoder using dual-path InfoNCE. Its top-$K$ molecules become
contextual priors for a cross-attention fusion module and ChemFormer decoder.
The decoder autoregressively generates a SMILES string, which is embedded and
compared with each pre-retrieved candidate to produce the final order
\cite{zhang2026glmr}. This converts cross-modal matching into molecular
similarity and yields strong MassSpecGym results. Its benefits, however, are
coupled to generation quality, decoding cost, and molecular validity.

\section{Problem Formulation}

Let $s$ be an MS/MS spectrum, $m^+$ its correct molecule, and
$\mathcal{D}_s$ a query-specific mass- or formula-conditioned candidate
library. A base retriever assigns

\begin{equation}
  b(s,m) = \operatorname{sim}(f_s(s), f_m(m))
\end{equation}

to every $m\in\mathcal{D}_s$ and returns the top-$K$ list
$\candidates_s=(m_1,\ldots,m_K)$. The reranker learns scores
$g(s,m_i,\candidates_s)$ such that $m^+$ is ordered as high as possible.
Because it cannot return a molecule outside $\candidates_s$, end-to-end
Recall@$k$ is upper-bounded by

\begin{equation}
  U_K = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}
  \mathbb{I}[m_n^+\in\candidates_{s_n}].
\end{equation}

We therefore report both $U_K$ and ranking metrics over all queries, assigning
zero credit when the positive is absent. This avoids reporting a conditional
accuracy only on successfully retrieved examples.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Overview}

The framework has three steps: cross-modal alignment, top-$K$ retrieval, and
supervised residual reranking. Spectrum and molecule encoders are frozen while
the reranker is trained, so their embeddings can be computed once and reused.

\subsection{Cross-Modal Base Retriever}

The spectrum encoder processes a sequence of peak $m/z$ values and intensities
with sinusoidal $m/z$ features and a Transformer. The molecular encoder is a
GINE network over atom and bond features. Mean-pooled graph features are
augmented with explicit graph-size features. Two projection heads map the
encoders to a common $d$-dimensional space:

\begin{equation}
  \zs = \frac{p_s(f_s(s))}{\lVert p_s(f_s(s))\rVert_2},
  \qquad
  \zm = \frac{p_m(f_m(m))}{\lVert p_m(f_m(m))\rVert_2}.
\end{equation}

We use a bidirectional contrastive loss that permits multiple positives.
Spectra and molecules sharing a molecular label form positives, which avoids
treating replicate spectra as false negatives. With a learned temperature
scale $a$, the base logits are

\begin{equation}
  \ell_{ij}=a\,\mathbf{z}_{s_i}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{m_j}.
\end{equation}

The loss averages the two retrieval directions over all positives in a batch.
At inference, the normalized dot product
$b_i=\zs^\top\mathbf{z}_{m_i}$ is the base score.

\subsection{Candidate Construction and Training Protocol}

For each query, the base retriever scores the corresponding MassSpecGym
candidate library and keeps its top $K=40$ molecules. Training lists must
contain a labeled positive for supervised learning. If the true training
molecule lies below rank 40, it replaces the last retrieved molecule while its
original base rank is retained. Candidate order is shuffled during reranker
training, but the base rank remains an explicit feature. This prevents the
tensor position itself from becoming a shortcut.

Positive insertion is restricted to the training split. Validation and test
caches are produced with insertion disabled; a missing positive remains missing
and lowers both the upper bound and every end-to-end metric. Candidate files are
loaded from the official MassSpecGym release. No record assigned to the test
split, nor any test target or label, is used for parameter optimization or
checkpoint selection in either stage.

\subsection{Residual Reranker Variants}

For candidate $m_i$ with base rank $r_i$, we construct

\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x}_i =
  [\zs,\ \mathbf{z}_{m_i},\ \zs\odot\mathbf{z}_{m_i},\
  |\zs-\mathbf{z}_{m_i}|,\ b_i,\ \mathbf{e}(r_i)],
  \label{eq:features}
\end{equation}

where $\mathbf{e}(r_i)$ is a learned rank embedding. The direct embeddings
retain modality-specific evidence, while product and absolute-difference terms
make symmetric matching cues explicit. An MLP projects each vector:

\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{h}_i=\operatorname{MLP}(\mathbf{x}_i).
\end{equation}

The candidate-set variant uses a Transformer encoder to exchange information
across candidates \cite{vaswani2017attention}:

\begin{equation}
  [\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_1,\ldots,\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_K]
  =\operatorname{Transformer}
  ([\mathbf{h}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{h}_K]).
\end{equation}

Padding masks support variable-sized lists. The matched pointwise control sets
$\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_i=\mathbf{h}_i$, retaining the same features, score head,
residual path, and training losses while removing all candidate interaction. A
score head predicts a correction $\Delta_i$, and the final score is residual:

\begin{equation}
  q_i=\alpha b_i+\Delta_i,
  \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}

where $\alpha$ is learned. The residual path lets the model preserve a useful
base order rather than relearn retrieval from scratch.

\subsection{Learning Objective}

Let $y$ be the positive index in a training list. The primary objective is
listwise cross-entropy:

\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{list}}
  =-\log\frac{\exp(q_y)}{\sum_{i=1}^{K}\exp(q_i)}.
\end{equation}

We also contrast the positive with every hard candidate using

\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pair}}
  =\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{i\ne y}
  \log\left(1+\exp(q_i-q_y+\gamma)\right).
\end{equation}

The complete objective is

\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}
  =\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{list}}
  +\lambda\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pair}}.
\end{equation}

For hidden width $h$, the set-aware variant adds $O(K^2h)$ attention cost over
the pointwise scorer. Neither variant decodes molecules token by token. An
empirical latency comparison with GLMR is not reported, so we make no measured
efficiency claim.

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Dataset and Candidate Libraries}

We use the official MassSpecGym split \cite{bushuiev2024massspecgym}: 194,119
training spectra, 19,429 validation spectra, and 17,556 test spectra, covering
32,010 unique molecules in total. Its structure-disjoint split groups similar
molecules and makes test structures substantially different from training
structures.

An exact audit of spectrum-input signatures (peak arrays, precursor $m/z$,
adduct, instrument, and collision energy) found no target-molecule duplicates
across splits, but six validation and three test signatures exactly matched
training signatures. In all nine matched pairs, the target molecules differ
and each evaluation query is marked \texttt{simulation\_challenge=true}. We
report these cases as cross-split spectrum-input overlap. In the canonical
pipeline reported below, all validation-dependent model selection excludes the
six affected validation spectra, leaving 19,423 validation queries for the
rerankers; alignment validation keys decrease from 3,386 to 3,384 molecular
entries. We retain and evaluate all 17,556 official test queries.

We evaluate both official retrieval settings. The mass-conditioned library uses
precursor mass and a tolerance window. The formula-conditioned library assumes
the molecular formula is available; it should therefore be interpreted as
formula-conditioned retrieval rather than unrestricted annotation. After base
top-40 truncation, test lists contain 40.00 candidates on average for the mass
setting and 35.73 for the formula setting.

\subsection{Implementation Details}

The aligned model uses 512-dimensional output embeddings. It does not load the
separately pretrained SpecEmbedding checkpoint;
the spectrum and molecular encoders are trained on the MassSpecGym training
split with bidirectional contrastive alignment. The canonical seed-42 alignment
run applies the validation exclusion above; it selects epoch 16 and stops at
epoch 21 after five epochs without improvement.

The reranker has hidden width 512, a 32-dimensional rank embedding, and dropout
0.1. The set-aware variant has two Transformer layers with eight attention
heads; the pointwise control removes both layers. We use AdamW with learning
rate $10^{-4}$, batch size 16, at most 30 epochs, and early stopping on
validation MRR with patience 5. The pairwise weight and margin are
$\lambda=0.2$ and $\gamma=0.1$. With one fixed alignment checkpoint and fixed
caches from the overlap-clean run, each reranker variant is trained with seeds
42, 43, and 44. This measures reranker variation, not end-to-end alignment
uncertainty. All models are selected without test-set access and evaluated on
one NVIDIA RTX 4090.

The top-40 base recall before supervised insertion is 93.78\% for mass and
90.42\% for formula on the training split. Training lists are completed to
100\% labeled coverage as described above. On test, insertion is disabled and
the corresponding upper bounds are 83.88\% and 89.26\%.

\subsection{Baselines and Metrics}

The controlled baselines are the same aligned retriever without the second-stage
ranker and a pointwise reranker that removes candidate self-attention while
retaining all other inputs and objectives. We also show JESTR and GLMR values
from Table 1 of the GLMR paper
\cite{zhang2026glmr}. These external numbers provide context but are not a
controlled ablation: JESTR and GLMR were not independently reproduced in our codebase, and
small differences in molecular canonicalization or candidate handling may
affect exact values.

We report Recall@1, Recall@5, Recall@20, and mean reciprocal rank (MRR) over all
test queries. MCES@1 measures structural distance between the top prediction and
target, so lower is better. Recall and MRR uncertainty is computed over three
reranker seeds with a fixed base checkpoint. MCES@1 for the overlap-clean base
and representative seed-42 Transformer checkpoints is pending and is therefore
not reported in the local rows below.

\section{Limitations and Responsible Evaluation}

\method{} cannot recover a molecule absent from the base top-$K$ list, so its
utility depends on first-stage recall. The formula-conditioned setting assumes
formula information that may itself require prediction in a real workflow. The
current external JESTR and GLMR rows are reported rather than independently
reproduced. The rerankers use three seeds, but all share one fixed seed-42
alignment checkpoint; the reported uncertainty therefore excludes first-stage
variation. Candidate self-attention shows a modest advantage in the clean run,
but that advantage is sensitive to the alignment checkpoint. These limitations
preclude a definitive state-of-the-art or self-attention benefit claim.

The canonical model-selection pipeline excludes all six audited
training--validation input overlaps. The clean and earlier pipelines select the
same alignment epochs, while their reranker checkpoint selections differ in 9
of 12 configurations. Since alignment was retrained rather than held bitwise
fixed, the observed old-to-clean differences must be interpreted as
pipeline-level sensitivity, not as a causal estimate for the six queries
alone. Multiple alignment seeds would be required to quantify first-stage
uncertainty.

Training-only positive insertion is a supervised sampling operation, not a
valid evaluation protocol. We expose the pre-insertion training recall and
disable insertion for validation and test. We also evaluate every test query,
including those whose positives are missing from the top-40 list. Finally, as a
closed-library reranker, the method cannot propose novel structures outside the
candidate database; this is a limitation relative to de novo generation, even
though it avoids generation validity failures.

\section{Conclusion}

We presented \method{} for MS/MS-based molecule retrieval. The framework uses
explicit pair features, retrieval priors, residual scoring, and listwise
supervision on top of a contrastively aligned base retriever. Controlled
MassSpecGym results show large gains from learned reranking in every one of
three reranker initializations on the fixed seed-42 base retriever under both
mass- and formula-conditioned libraries. A matched three-seed comparison finds
that the candidate-set Transformer has a modest advantage over the pointwise
scorer and wins all six paired comparisons on the overlap-clean checkpoint.
Because this pattern differs from the earlier alignment checkpoint, the
strongest supported conclusion is the value of direct residual learning to
rank; a checkpoint-robust benefit from self-attention remains unestablished.
Claims about matched external baselines, individual feature and loss
contributions, and measured efficiency are outside the present evidence.
